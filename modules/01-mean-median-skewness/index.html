Mean, median, skewness, and outliers

From central tendency to distribution shape: why outliers affect the mean more than the median.

Created by: Mahdi Akbarzadeh
Visual explanation
R activity

Learning goals

  • Distinguish between mean and median as measures of central tendency.
  • Explain why the mean is sensitive to extreme values.
  • Recognize when median-based summaries are more appropriate for skewed medical variables.
  • Connect numeric summaries with the shape of a histogram.

Teaching notes

  • The mean uses every value and is pulled by extreme observations.
  • The median is based on rank order and is more resistant to extreme values.
  • For right-skewed variables, median and IQR often describe the typical patient better than mean and SD.
  • A summary measure should be chosen after looking at the distribution.
Common mistake: Treating the mean as the best description of a typical patient even when the data are strongly skewed.

R activity

The activity simulates systolic blood pressure values, adds one extreme value, and compares how the mean and median change.

The dataset is created inside the R script. Students can run the file directly without opening a separate data file.
